How many views on TikTok to get paid? (2026 thresholds)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published October 15, 2026 · 7 min read

To get paid for views on TikTok, you need 100,000 views in the last 30 days plus 10,000 followers (Creator Rewards Program, official page updated September 11, 2026). After that, with a US audience, it takes roughly 2,500 to 5,000 views to earn $1 and 250,000 to 500,000 views to earn $100. That math only works for videos longer than 1 minute, at the rates US creators report in 2026.

In short
  • Three view thresholds matter: 100,000 views in 30 days to join, 1,000 For You views per video, and 5 seconds of watch time for a view to count.
  • 1 million views on a video over 1 minute pays about $200 to $400 with a US audience. Under 1 minute, it pays nothing.
  • 95% of the viral videos over 1 minute I analyzed passed 100,000 views. One of them covers the monthly requirement by itself.
  • 75% passed 500,000 views, the point where a video earns $100 even at the low end of the rate range.

The three view counts TikTok checks

TikTok has three view thresholds, and most guides only mention the first. That one gets you into the program: 100,000 views over the last 30 days, alongside 10,000 followers and being 18 or older. The other two apply video by video once you're in.

Sources: TikTok Creator Academy, Creator Rewards Program page (updated September 11, 2026) and qualified views page (August 18, 2026).

The country rule hits harder than people expect. Views from Canada, Australia or India pay nothing, because those countries aren't on the list. If a big slice of your audience lives outside the 8, your payout shrinks before any rate is applied.

How many views for $1, $100 or $1,000

With a US audience, 1,000 displayed views pay about 20 to 40 cents. Two 2026 numbers drive that. US creators on Reddit report $0.50 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views. And 30% to 50% of an established creator's views qualify, so I used 40%.

The same video earns roughly six times more when it reaches Americans. TikTok says so itself on its audience commercial value page (August 2026): viewer age and language change the payout. To run your own numbers, plug your views and video length into the TikTok money calculator.

Your view count is not your paid view count

TikTok pays on qualified views, and those are often less than half of what your counter shows. A view counts if it lasts 5 seconds or more, comes from one of the 8 countries, isn't a rewatch from the same account and doesn't come from an ad.

In an r/TikTokMonetizing poll from August 13, 2026, established creators reported 30% to 50% qualified views. A new account with a mostly US and UK audience shared something much worse on August 5, 2026: 8,800 qualified views out of 289,600, or 3%. About 77% of its views lasted under 5 seconds.

So the first two seconds of your video are worth actual money. A hook that holds people past 5 seconds raises your paid share without adding a single view to the counter.

How far viral videos over 1 minute actually go

Almost every viral video over 1 minute passes 100,000 views, and three in four pass 500,000. I looked at about 210 viral videos longer than a minute, each analyzed one by one, and checked which view count each one cleared.

Half of the viral videos over 1 minute stayed under 1.1 million views. At 2026 US rates, that typical viral video would earn $220 to $440 from Creator Rewards.

English-language videos look about the same. Among roughly 135 of them over a minute, 70% passed 500,000 views and the one in the middle landed at 1.03 million. So a US creator needs two to five videos at that level each month to clear $1,000.

92 million views, zero dollars

Length decides whether you get paid at all, more than views do. Two English-language videos from my data show the gap. A 17-second Reel from the account Otai / study with me, posted November 15, 2025, pulled 92 million views. Its on-screen hook:

"studying until the ice melts"
Otai / study with me, Reel from November 15, 2025, 92 million views on Instagram.

On TikTok, that video would earn nothing from Creator Rewards: it's under a minute. Compare a 66-second Reel by Davide De Pierro from August 10, 2025, with 25.3 million views. With a US audience on TikTok, my math puts it at $5,000 to $10,000. Short videos clear 1 million views a bit more often in my data (61% vs 52% for videos over a minute), but they never get paid.

Views vs brand deals for small creators

In the US, views and brand deals pay in the same ballpark for small creators. Influencer Marketing Hub puts a TikTok micro-influencer (10,000 to 100,000 followers) at $200 to $800 per video (updated July 7, 2026). At US Creator Rewards rates, that equals 500,000 to 4 million views.

The difference is reliability. A brand pays the agreed price whether the video flops or not, while Creator Rewards swings month to month. One US home and family creator reported dropping from $1.00 to $0.03 per 1,000 qualified views for a year before recovering in August 2026. For the follower side of the question, see how many followers you need to get paid on TikTok, and for every income stream, the guide on how to make money on TikTok.

My take

If your audience is American, make videos of 65 to 90 seconds and treat Creator Rewards as real income. A typical viral video over a minute earns $220 to $440 in my math, and 75% of the ones I analyzed cleared the $100 mark. That's worth building a format around. I wouldn't depend on it alone, though: some creators have watched their rate fall from $1.00 to $0.03 per 1,000 qualified views, and a brand deal pays the same whatever the algorithm does. The limit: my videos come mostly from Instagram, and the rates come from creator reports, not from TikTok, which publishes none.

Frequently asked questions

How much does TikTok pay for 1 million views?

About $200 to $400 with a US audience, if the video runs over 1 minute. Under a minute, nothing from Creator Rewards. The full payout rules are in the guide to the TikTok Creator Rewards Program.

Do I need 100,000 views on every video?

No. The 100,000 views are counted across your whole account over 30 days. Each paid video only needs 1,000 views from the For You feed.

Is 100,000 views viral?

For an account under 20,000 followers, usually yes. For a big account, it depends on its usual numbers, which I break down in how many views counts as viral.
