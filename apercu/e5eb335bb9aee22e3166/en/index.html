Brand deals for creators: what to charge ($150 to $3,400 for a small account) and how to get them

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 7, 2026 · 9 min read

For your first brand deal, charge about $20 per 1,000 typical views, so $400 if your usual video gets 20,000 views. In 2026, creators with 10K to 100K followers get $150 to $500 for an Instagram feed post and $200 to $800 for a TikTok video (Influencer Marketing Hub). To land the deal, pitch the brands already paying creators in your niche, and lead with a video idea, not your follower count.

In short
  • TikTok publishes no official rate. Small-account prices run from about $150 to $3,400 depending on who is buying.
  • In nearly 500 viral videos I broke down, I could identify 8 brand deals. Their engagement rate is 2.4%, against 6.2% for everything else.
  • Among those 8, videos that open on a topic got 2.3 million views. Videos that open on the brand got 110,000.
  • When a brand wants your video for its ads, creators who price by percentage charge 15% to 35% of their base rate per 30 days (Aspire, February 2026).
  • The FTC says a free product counts as a connection you must disclose, and a platform's paid-partnership tool alone may not be enough.

A creator with 10K to 100K followers usually sells a post for $150 to $800 on their own. The biggest jump comes from the type of buyer, and the rate cards below show it.

Price yourself on your typical views

Your starting rate fits on one line: typical views, divided by 1,000, times $20. Sort your last 10 videos by views and take the 5th. That video looks most like what the brand will actually get.

The $20 comes from the low end of one specific range. A US agency buyer says they pay $0.02 to $0.10 per expected view (Reddit, June 2026). One testimony is thin, but the math lands inside the published rate cards. At 20,000 views you get $400 to $2,000.

Then check against followers. Influencer Marketing Hub cites $10 to $20 per 1,000 followers, or $300 to $600 at 30,000 followers. If your views top your follower count, charge on views. Small accounts have the edge here: among the viral videos I broke down, the middle video gets 36 times the followers at 10K to 50K, and 0.7 times above 1 million.

Sponsored videos get 2.6 times less engagement

The 8 identified brand deals sit at 2.4% engagement, against 6.2% for the other 488 videos. They collect 16 likes per 1,000 views, compared with 40. Under 2% of the set carries a visible brand at all.

The clean test compares a creator's sponsored videos with their other videos, and I only have one such case. Betty, 99,272 followers, has a dating story at 6 million views and 7.5% engagement. Betty's #ad video for NYX got 55,367 views and 1.15%. A festival video featuring Pepsi got 81,221 views and 1.7%.

Same account, same month (September 2025): the story got 74 to 109 times the views of the two brand videos.

Don't treat that as a law. The story is the account's best video, and videos enter my set because they took off. The practical point still holds: never sell a brand your record, and don't promise your usual engagement rate.

Open on the topic, not the brand

Where the brand shows up moves views far more than engagement. I sorted the 8 videos by hand. Four open on a topic and bring the brand in later, or leave it in the caption. The other four put the product front and center from the start.

Rachel Martinez, 86,353 followers, argues that you should keep interviewing even when you like your job, then works in the tool Cluely. That video hit 3.9 million views, or 45 times the account's followers. Max, 54,507 followers, runs a sketch about which bus you should date, and the answer is FlixBus: 2.1 million views.

Now compare the NYX hook, which gives everything away in one breath:

"It is 7 am. I've got 12 NYX Professional Makeup Smushy Lipsticks. And all week I'm gonna match them to my outfit." Betty, September 2025

That reel reached 0.6 times the account's followers. All three brand-first videos with a known follower count stayed under it. Engagement stayed low in both groups, at 2.4% and 2.3%, so a topic gets you reach and won't rescue the likes.

What to charge on top of the post

Your post price buys one publication on your account. Aspire surveyed nearly 900 marketers and creators (February 2026): 77% of brands reuse creator content in paid ads, and 67% build that right into the first contract or rate. Find that line before you sign.

What the brand asks forReported add-onSource
Running your video as a paid ad15% to 35% of base rate per 30 days, or a flat $500 to $1,000 extensionAspire, February 2026
Boosting from your handle (TikTok Spark Ads)20% to 50% of base rateKolsquare, July 2026

Category exclusivityraises the fee, no reliable figure publishedAspire, Influencer Marketing Hub

Add-ons as a share of the post price or in US dollars. Self-reported ranges, not an official rate card.

Many brands pay little for this part. In Aspire's survey, 43% of those paying extra for usage pay under $500. Example: a $400 post plus 30 days of ad use at 25% comes to $500. For exclusivity, name the category and the end date, then price the deals you would have to turn down.

How to land your first brand deal

The shortest route is a cold pitch to brands already spending in your niche. CreatorIQ surveyed 5,055 creators in 2026, and brands put creator fit at the top of their criteria. Your email needs to prove fit in five lines.

  1. Find ten videos in your niche labeled "Paid partnership" or tagged #ad, and list the brands. They already have a budget.
  2. Get your typical views and your rate from the engagement rate calculator. Benchmark against your niche: at 10K to 500K followers, the middle viral video gets 8.2% in personal development and 5.2% in creative and content niches.
  3. Pitch one video idea where the topic comes first and the product second.
  4. On TikTok, also apply to TikTok One. A July 2026 help page says 1,000 followers for projects, while a support page says 10,000 to join. The eligibility check in the app settles it.

A sample email to adapt: "Hi Sam, I make 30-second budget travel videos for people with two weeks of PTO. My videos usually get 20,000 views at 6% engagement. Idea for your luggage: I show why carry-ons get gate-checked, and your bag shows up at second 15 as the fix. Want my rate?"

Then go after video two. The Influencer Marketing Factory looked at more than 300,000 promoted posts (May 2026) and found 63% of brand and creator relationships stop at one deal. Long-running ambassador posts make up nearly 20% on YouTube and under 3% on TikTok.

Disclosure and paperwork

You have to disclose every deal, including free products. The FTC's guide for influencers (November 2019) says financial relationships "aren't limited to money". It also warns: "Don't assume that a platform's disclosure tool is good enough." Put the disclosure with the endorsement itself, never behind a "more" link or at the end of the video.

Plenty of creators skip this. The same Influencer Marketing Factory report found that less than a third of Instagram promoted posts were properly disclosed.

Get the deal in writing, even by email. France made written contracts mandatory from January 1, 2026 for deals of €1,000 or more in a year, under a November 2025 decree. Its list makes a good checklist anywhere: who the parties are, the exact deliverables, the fee, the value of any free product, and who owns what, including ad usage.

My take

Sell a flat fee and a format idea, and never sell a view count. In my 8 videos, views range from 55,000 to 3.9 million. Nobody can guarantee a result that swings that much, and a pay-per-view deal moves all of that risk onto you. Push back on the brief as well. If the brand insists on its name in the hook, show them the gap: 110,000 views against 2.3 million. My limit: this is 8 videos, mostly Instagram, picked from a set of videos that already did well and sorted by hand. That is enough to shape your next brief. It's not enough to promise anyone a number.

Questions creators ask

How much should I charge with 10K followers?

Start at $150 to $800 per post, based on your typical views and platform. On YouTube, expect from about $500 per integration. Influencer Marketing Hub publishes those ranges, and an agency working for a large brand can go higher.

Should I take free product instead of money?

Only for something you already want, and only once or twice. A free product doesn't pay for your time, and the FTC still treats it as a connection you have to disclose.

Do sponsored TikToks earn Creator Rewards too?

No. The Creator Academy page on the program rules out sponsored videos, and TikTok's European terms (April 2025) exclude any "advertisement, paid promotion, or sponsored content".
