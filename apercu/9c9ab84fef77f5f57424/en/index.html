Why did my TikTok views drop? The real causes (46% of same-week video pairs are 2x apart)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 25, 2026 · 10 min read

Your TikTok views most likely dropped because of the video itself, not a penalty. Across 24 accounts that all had a viral video, two videos posted in the same week were 2x or more apart in views 46% of the time. On top of that, TikTok as a whole lost views per video: Metricool measured a 31% drop between early 2025 and early 2026, while the number of videos posted rose 72% (May 2026).

In short
  • Big swings between videos are normal, even for accounts that go viral: in the same week, close to half of video pairs are 2x or more apart.
  • Posted less than a month apart, two videos with a similar format were 5x apart only 2% of the time. With a different format: 23%.
  • Views per video fell 31% across TikTok from January–February 2025 to January–February 2026 (Metricool, 2.3 million posts).
  • In the US, the late-January 2026 "0 views" scare after the ownership change was a data center outage, per TikTok's US joint venture.
  • A real restriction is visible: TikTok shows it in the video's analytics. No notice means you should look at your formats.

A drop from one video to the next is normal

Even viral accounts swing between huge videos and average ones, sometimes a day apart. I took the 24 accounts for which I have at least three dated videos among the viral videos I analyzed: 116 videos posted from July 2024 to June 2026. Then I compared every video with every other video from the same account.

Of the 41 pairs posted within 7 days of each other, 46% were 2x or more apart. In 12% of them, one video got 5x the views of the other. The algorithm doesn't "change" in a week. The difference comes from the video.

Ali Woods, a comedy and lifestyle creator with 395,854 followers, shows it well. On February 16, 2026, he posted an 18-second video with the on-screen text "Every middle-class person age 25-35." It got 13.1 million views. The next day, a 62-second video titled "How they created Wagamama" got 1.5 million. Nine times fewer, 24 hours later, with no penalty involved.

My guide on how to get unshadowbanned on TikTok shows the same pattern from another angle: for these accounts, 36% of videos got less than half the views of the one before.

The 2026 slump hit all of TikTok

Part of your drop comes from the platform, not from you. Metricool, a social media management tool, compared 2,314,756 posts from more than 92,000 accounts worldwide between January–February 2025 and January–February 2026. Its results, published May 12, 2026:

The math is simple. There are far more videos and not 72% more viewers to watch them, so each video gets fewer views on average. If you're down around 30% over a year, you're tracking the platform. If you lost 80% in two weeks, something else is going on.

The January 2026 "0 views" scare in the US

If your drop started in late January 2026 and you're in the US, a technical outage played a part. TikTok's US business moved to the new TikTok USDS joint venture in January 2026. Days later, many creators saw "0" views on their posts or no earnings in their creator accounts, and some blamed a new algorithm.

The joint venture gave a different cause: a power outage at one of its US data center partner sites. On February 2, 2026, Social Media Today reported that all TikTok US servers were back up. If your numbers have stayed low since then, the outage no longer explains it.

What explains the gap: format, more than timing

Two videos from the same account with a similar format get similar views. To measure it, I compared four traits of each video. Its story structure (problem then fix, list, question then answer...). Its emotional driver (relatable, useful, debate, funny). What carries it (the person, the words, or the visual format). And its length.

I kept only the 92 pairs posted 30 days apart or less, from 22 accounts. Over a span that short, the account, its audience and the algorithm barely move.

When two videos share 3 or 4 traits, the middle pair is 1.3x apart. When they share 0 or 1, the gap grows to 2.3x, and one pair in four is more than 5x apart.

Less than a month apart, two videos with a similar format were 5x apart in only 2% of cases. Change the format and it jumps to 23%.

Davide De Pierro, a lifestyle creator with 2.8 million followers, sticks to one format: a short confession about adult life that ends on a lesson, about a minute long. His three Reels from March 11, 18 and 19, 2026 got 5.0 million, 6.1 million and 4.6 million views. Same format, same ballpark.

Story structure matters at every time scale

Of the four traits, story structure separates videos best, whatever the time between them. Keeping the same structure narrows the gap over one month and over six.

Read it row by row. Within 30 days, switching structure makes a 5x gap more than four times as likely (3% vs. 14%). Past 90 days, gaps grow for everyone, but they stay smaller when the structure stays the same. If your last video flopped, check its structure first, then the structure of your best video this month.

Over several months, your account changes too

Timing barely matters week to week, but it does over months. The middle pair is 1.8x apart within the same week and 1.6x apart between 8 and 30 days. Past 90 days, it reaches 4.4x.

A lot moves in six months: your topics, your audience, the competition in your niche, the 2026 slump. One limit of my data also plays a part. Views were recorded on a given date, so older videos had more time to pile them up. The practical rule: if your drop is a week old, look at the video. If it has lasted three months, compare your formats from three months ago with today's.

Useful-tip videos lose two matchups out of three

When an account switches emotional driver, the "useful tip" video is often the weaker one. I took every pair of videos from the same account built on two different drivers and counted which one won.

  • Debate ("what do you think?"): wins 35 of 52 matchups, or 67%.
  • Relatable ("that's so me"): wins 52 of 86, or 60%.
  • Useful tip: wins 24 of 72, or 33%.

That doesn't mean you should drop tips: a third of the viral videos I analyzed rely on them. It means that if your account alternates tips and relatable videos, your dips will land on the tips more often. Your "drop" might just be a more educational week than the one before. To see which drivers get shared, read how the TikTok algorithm works.

Your past hit doesn't carry the next video

TikTok doesn't recommend you because you've had views before. In its official For You explainer (June 18, 2020), TikTok writes that "neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors" in recommendations. Each video is judged on what the people who see it do.

That's why the drop feels so harsh after a viral video. Your 2-million-view video lends nothing to the next one. If the next one changes structure, driver and length, you start over with a format your audience hasn't backed yet. Compare yourself to your usual level: how many views counts as viral gives the thresholds by account size.

When the drop really is a restriction

A real restriction leaves a trace you can read. Under its Community Guidelines in force since September 24, 2026, TikTok shows in a video's analytics when it has been made ineligible for the For You feed. For a whole account, it sends a notification.

Run three checks. Open the analytics for your last 4 videos and look for a restriction notice. Check the share of views from the For You feed: across TikTok, it drives 72.7% of views, per Metricool. If it falls close to zero on all your recent videos, take it seriously. Then go through your notifications.

If a video is stuck at exactly 0 views, the cause is usually technical or moderation-related, covered in why your TikTok has 0 views.

What to do tomorrow morning

This takes 20 minutes and starts from your own videos.

  1. List your last 10 videos and their views. Sort them from lowest to highest and keep the one in the middle: that's your usual level.
  2. For your best and your weakest, write four words: structure, driver, what carries it, length.
  3. Count the traits that changed. Three or four? Your drop comes from the format, not from TikTok.
  4. Make your next video with the structure and driver of your best video this month, on a new topic.
  5. To compare videos on equal terms, run them through the engagement rate calculator.

My take

When your TikTok views drop out of nowhere, stop blaming the calendar. A week changes almost nothing: in my data, two videos posted less than a month apart are 1.3x apart when the format is similar and 2.3x when it changes. Format explains more than timing. My position: before you talk about a shadowban or a "bad stretch," put the video that tanked next to your best one this month and compare them trait by trait. The limit: my numbers come from 24 accounts that had already gone viral, mostly on Instagram, and I don't have their watch time or For You traffic. The 2026 slump (down 31%, per Metricool) is real, and no format cancels it.

Common questions

How long does a TikTok views drop last?

TikTok publishes no timeline for a normal dip in reach. In my data, the gap between two videos from the same account is about the same in the first week and the first month (1.8x, then 1.6x). A format-driven drop ends when you go back to a format that works.

Will posting more often bring my views back?

Nothing shows it. Across TikTok, Metricool measured 72% more videos in early 2026 and 31% fewer views per video. Posting more videos in a format that isn't working just adds flops.

Should I delete a video that got few views?

No, unless TikTok flags it as ineligible. A weak video doesn't take views from the next ones: according to TikTok, an account's past performance isn't a direct recommendation factor.
