How many views is viral? Real thresholds by account size (43% of viral videos stay under 1M)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published October 15, 2026 · 8 min read

TikTok has no official viral threshold. The usual benchmark is 1 million views, but the real bar depends on account size. Across nearly 390 viral videos where I know the creator's follower count, three out of four top 15 times their followers under 100K followers, 1.1 times between 100K and 1M, and 0.28 times above 1M (Prooven data, 2023 to July 2026).

In short
  • 43% of the viral videos I analyzed never reached 1 million views. Among English-language videos, it's 44%.
  • The "5x your followers" rule holds for small accounts: 82% of viral videos from accounts under 100K pass it. Above 1M followers, only 12% do.
  • Viral videos land in the same range whatever the account size: 55% cross 1 million views both under 100K followers and between 100K and 1M.
  • The bigger a video gets, the fewer comments it gets per view: 2.3 per 1,000 views at 10K to 100K views, 0.3 above 5 million.

The five view tiers, from flop to mega-viral

I sort every video into five fixed view tiers. Under 10K views is a flop. 10K to 100K is a normal video. 100K to 1M is a good one. 1M to 5M is viral, and anything above 5M is mega-viral.

TikTok didn't set these tiers. I use them to sort nearly 500 videos I picked because they took off, then broke down one by one. Here is the part most creators miss: 35% of these viral videos stopped between 100K and 1M views. Put the bar at 1 million and you write off more than a third of the videos that actually went viral.

Across more than 1,500 viral videos in the Prooven database, half got 703,000 views or fewer. One in four stayed under 174,000 views (snapshot from July 29, 2026).

Why every site gives you a different number

Each site picks its own threshold, and none of them shows data. Techweez says a small account can celebrate 10K to 50K views. Virlo, a video analytics tool, uses the 5 to 10 times your followers rule. Both are partly right because they're describing different accounts.

TikTok itself gives no number. In its June 2020 explainer on the For You feed, it states that follower count is not a direct factor in recommendations. The viral bar is a convention creators made up.

How many views is viral for your account size

The bigger your account, the higher the bar in raw views and the lower it gets as a multiple of followers. I matched nearly 390 viral videos with the follower count of 284 accounts. For each size, the table gives the floor: the number three viral videos out of four exceed.

In practice, with 20K followers, a video starts to count as viral around 300K views, about 15 times your audience. With 2 million followers, a video at 600K views can already be a hit, even though only a third of your followers saw it.

The 5x rule only works for small accounts

The rule holds under 100K followers and then falls apart. Below that line, 82% of viral videos got at least 5 times more views than the account has followers. Between 100K and 1M, 44% did. Above 1M, 12%.

Look only at videos that crossed a million views, and 30% of those from accounts with over 1M followers still got fewer views than the account has followers. Take a Gary Vaynerchuk Reel from November 2025: 1.8 million views on an account with 11.3 million followers. That's 0.16x, and nobody would call it a miss.

Small accounts blow past every ratio. One account with 30K followers got 28 million views on a 7-second Reel, more than 900 times its audience. It opens on this on-screen text:

"In Japan, Laziness is considered a Disease and people treat it with these 7 methods"

Zoe Emma Hill, Reel from December 15, 2025

So on a small account, aim for at least 5 times your followers. On a big one, compare the video with your own recent posts. The ratio stops meaning anything.

In English, 1 million is a fair bar

English-language videos reach a much bigger pool of viewers, and it shows. Across nearly 340 English-language viral videos, 56% passed 1 million views and 20% passed 5 million. Half of them topped 1.3 million.

The picture changes in smaller languages. Across nearly 70 French-language viral videos, only 43% crossed 1 million, and 6% passed 5 million. If you post in English for a US or UK audience, the 1 million benchmark is realistic for a video that truly takes off. If you post in a smaller language, viral can mean 300K.

Views and paid views are two different counts. TikTok's Creator Rewards Program only pays on qualified views, which must last at least 5 seconds, and only on videos longer than one minute (more in the guide to the Creator Rewards Program). To see what a viral video could earn, run it through the TikTok money calculator.

What separates a 100K video from a 5M one

The videos that climb highest get the most shares and the fewest comments. I compared the middle video of each tier. Shares go from 7.8 per 1,000 views (10K to 100K views) to 13.5 (over 5 million). Comments move the opposite way.

A mega-viral video gets 8 times fewer comments per view than a normal one. It reaches strangers who watch, share and scroll on. If your video has lots of comments but few shares, it's talking to your community. It probably won't leave your bubble.

Two more gaps stand out. The middle mega-viral video runs 36 seconds, against 62 seconds for good videos. And 57% of mega-viral videos follow a format anyone can reuse as is, against 42% of good ones.

How to tell if your video went viral for your account

The right yardstick is your own account. Here's the check I'd run, using the numbers above.

  1. Write down your follower count and the views on your last 10 videos from your TikTok analytics.
  2. Under 100K followers: your video is viral at 5 times your followers, and clearly viral at 15 times.
  3. Between 100K and 1M: aim for at least as many views as you have followers.
  4. Above 1M: compare it with your recent videos. The ratio no longer helps.
  5. Then check shares per 1,000 views. Above 13, your video travels like the ones that pass a million.

The engagement rate calculator does the math for you. And if your videos aren't getting any traction at all, start with why a TikTok gets 0 views.

My take

Stop measuring yourself against a million. For a small account, 5 times your followers already means the algorithm pushed your video to strangers, and 82% of the small-account viral videos I analyzed cleared that line. Once you're there, watch shares more than comments, because shares are what separate a good video from a multimillion one. The limit: I only have videos that worked, mostly Instagram Reels, and follower counts were recorded after the fact. I also don't know how many days those views took.

Frequently asked questions

How many views in 24 hours is viral?

TikTok publishes no daily threshold, and my data doesn't track view speed. The sites quoting 1 million in 24 or 72 hours cite no data. Your best check is comparing the video with your last 10 over the same time span.

Is 100K views viral?

For an account under 20K followers, yes: that's at least 5 times the audience, the line 82% of small-account viral videos pass. For an account with 500K followers, no. The guide on how to go viral on TikTok covers how to reach the next tier.

How many followers do you gain from a viral video?

I don't have a reliable number: I recorded follower counts once, after the video. If your goal is the payout threshold, see how many followers you need to get paid.
