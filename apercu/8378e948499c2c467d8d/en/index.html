How to reset your TikTok algorithm (and why 88% of viral creators only hit once)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 16, 2026 · 8 min read

To reset your TikTok algorithm, go to your profile, tap the menu in the top right, then Settings and privacy, Content preferences, and Refresh your For You feed. TikTok then shows you popular videos "as if you just signed up for TikTok" (announcement of March 16, 2023). You can't undo it. Your follows and settings stay put, and it does nothing to the reach of your own videos.

In short
  • The official button is called Refresh your For You feed. It only resets the For You feed: your Following feed, profile, inbox and ads are untouched, per TikTok's help center.
  • Lighter options come first: Not interested, keyword filters (up to 200 words since June 2025) and Manage topics.
  • No official TikTok page presents clearing your cache or starting a new account as a way to change your recommendations.
  • For creators, the real use is research. In the viral videos I analyzed, 88% of creators had just one viral video. A useful feed follows dozens of accounts in your niche, not three big names.

How to refresh your For You feed, step by step

It takes under a minute on iPhone or Android. You do it from the settings menu, not from the feed:

  1. Tap Profile in the bottom right.
  2. Tap the menu icon (three lines, top right).
  3. Open Settings and privacy.
  4. Tap Content preferences.
  5. Tap Refresh your For You feed and follow the prompts.

TikTok's help page mentions a second route in select regions: the "Why this video" option on a video in your For You feed. Menu labels shift between app versions. If the button is missing, update the app first. TikTok announced the feature on March 16, 2023, and the rollout didn't reach every account on day one.

What a refresh wipes, and what it keeps

It wipes what TikTok learned about your taste for the For You feed. Nothing else. The 2023 announcement puts it plainly: you see your feed "as if you just signed up." Popular videos replace your usual picks, and the way you react to them (likes, shares, watching to the end) rebuilds your feed from there.

What stays: the accounts you follow, your Following feed, your profile, your inbox and your ads, per the help page. Settings you already turned on stay on too, like filtered keywords and muted accounts. There is no undo button. If your feed already works for you 80% of the time, a full reset costs more than it fixes.

Lighter fixes to try before a full reset

Most of the time, one targeted setting does the job. TikTok's "Making your feed For You" page describes several:

  • Not interested: press and hold a video, then tap it. You'll see fewer videos like it.
  • Filter keywords: since June 3, 2025, up to 200 keywords per account, and TikTok's AI also catches synonyms and slang.
  • Manage topics: dial a topic up or down. TikTok says this never removes a topic entirely.
  • Mute an account: its posts leave your feed without you blocking anyone.
  • Restricted Mode, to limit content that isn't suitable for everyone.

One more thing if you're in the EU: since August 2023, the Digital Services Act requires TikTok to offer a non-personalized For You feed based on what's popular where you live. US users don't get that switch. In the US, the refresh button is the closest thing to a blank slate.

Clearing your cache or making a new account won't fix it

The cache is temporary storage on your phone. Clearing it frees up space. None of the official pages I read for this article present it as a way to change your recommendations. Deleting and reinstalling the app doesn't help either, because your history lives on your account, not your phone.

A new account does give you a blank feed, but you lose your followers in the process, and you gain no reach. TikTok has said since June 18, 2020, that follower count and past high-performing videos are not "direct factors" in recommendations. A fresh account doesn't start with a cleaner record than an old one. If your views just dropped off a cliff, the guide on shadowbans helps you tell a real restriction from a normal dip.

For creators: your feed isn't your reach

Refreshing your viewer feed won't bring your views back. TikTok describes a For You feed built for each viewer from what that viewer does. Your video is judged on how strangers react when it's shown to them. No official page links what you watch to how far your own posts travel. The full breakdown is in the guide on how the TikTok algorithm works.

A reset still has a real use for you, though. Your feed is your research desk. If you post about personal finance and your feed is all cats, recipes and NBA highlights, you never see the formats that win in your niche. Right after a refresh, TikTok serves popular videos on every topic. What you search, follow and watch to the end in those first days decides what comes back.

Follow lots of small accounts, not three big names

A niche's viral videos come from many different creators. Across nearly 500 viral videos, I count more than 330 creators, and 88% of them have exactly one. The most frequent creator in the whole set has 8.

Most viral videos in a niche come from accounts that went viral once. Follow only the three biggest names in your niche and you miss nearly everything that works.

So after the refresh, search the words your audience types. Watch the high-view videos in your niche to the end, and follow the small accounts that hit big. Scroll past everything else, and tap Not interested when an off-topic subject keeps coming back. Save the videos whose format you could reuse: they're your templates. Before you borrow a structure, check that it holds an audience with the engagement rate calculator.

My take

Only refresh your feed if off-topic content has taken it over, and do it for research, never for views. TikTok doesn't link your viewer feed to your reach anywhere. What you watch every day does shape what you post, though. The data tells you what to look for: in every niche, two structures cover more than half the viral videos, and 88% of creators went viral only once. A good creator feed follows dozens of small accounts in your niche. The limit: my data is mostly Instagram Reels, and I only measure videos that worked, not the ones that flopped with the same structure. Frequently asked questions

How long does it take for the feed to rebuild?

TikTok doesn't publish a timeline. Your feed starts shifting with your first interactions, but no official source puts a number of days on it.

Can you undo a refresh?

No. TikTok's help page says the refresh can't be reversed once it's done.

Will my followers know I reset my feed?

No. The setting only touches your own For You feed. Your profile, videos and followers stay exactly as they were. To see how your videos reach strangers, read the guide on the TikTok FYP.
