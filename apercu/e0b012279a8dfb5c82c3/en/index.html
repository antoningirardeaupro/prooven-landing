Hook examples: 50 viral video hook templates (real data)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 27, 2026 · 15 min read

The hooks that work best talk to the viewer, not about the creator, and drop them into a scene with the first word. I transcribed about 250 spoken hooks from viral videos word for word (2023 to July 2026, mostly Instagram Reels). Hooks that open with "if" get 2.7 million views for the middle video and twice the average share rate. The 50 hook examples below all come from real videos, each with its view count and a link.

In short
  • Hooks that open with "if" get 20.7 shares per 1,000 views, double the average of 10.3. Only 6% of hooks use it.
  • A hook that says "you" and never "I" gets 1.7 times the shares of one that says "I" and never "you" (11.9 vs 6.9 per 1,000 views).
  • Numbers in a hook drive comments (0.63 per 1,000 views vs 0.40 without), not views.
  • Text-only hooks, written on screen but never said out loud, get 0.4 million views. Spoken hooks get almost three times more.
  • The 50 templates are grouped by what they do: scene, contrarian claim, question, command, number, contrast, call-out, confession.

What viral hooks have in common

Viral hooks are about you, the viewer, and what is about to happen to you. I took every spoken hook I had transcribed and tagged its shape. Does it open with "if"? Does it say "you" or "I"? Does it hold a number or a question? Then I compared views, shares and comments across groups. The gaps are big enough to act on.

"If" hooks get twice the shares

Starting with "if" doubles the share rate: 20.7 per 1,000 views, against 10.3 for all hooks. The word forces your brain to play the scene ("if you drop garlic into a jar of honey…"). You picture yourself doing it. Then you think of the friend who should see it too.

The group is small: 16 hooks. But it spans very different niches, from nutrition (Cory Rodriguez, 9.4 million views) to friendship (taryn arnold scott, 23.9 million) to science (Cleo Abram). Shares are what Instagram watches. In his ranking explainer of May 31, 2023, Adam Mosseri lists the chance you reshare a reel first among the predictions that matter most.

Say "you," not "I"

Hooks aimed at the viewer get more views and far more shares. The ones that say "you" and never "I" get 1.3 million views and 11.9 shares per 1,000 views. Flip it, "I" and never "you," and you drop to 1.0 million views and 6.9 shares.

Swapping "I" for "you" in your first line goes with 1.7 times the shares: 11.9 per 1,000 views, against 6.9.

A stranger scrolling has no reason to care about your life yet. People share what speaks to their own life, or to a friend's. The confessions that do work in the list below (Davide De Pierro, Ali Woods) say "I," but they talk about something everyone lives: getting older, paying taxes, cooking the same dinner again.

Numbers get comments, not views

A number in the hook pulls comments without pulling views. Hooks like "10 habits that…" or "3 years of therapy in 1 minute" get 0.63 comments per 1,000 views, against 0.40 for hooks without one. On views, they land at 1.0 million, below the 1.3 million of hooks without a number.

Questions behave the same way: 0.59 comments per 1,000 views, against 0.41 without a question mark. A number or a question gives people something to argue with or add to. Want a busy comment section? Pick one of those templates. Want reach? Open with an "if" scene.

Text-only hooks get a third of the views

Videos that only show text on screen, with no spoken line, get 0.4 million views for the middle video. Videos with a spoken hook get 1.1 million. Text-only videos also get half the shares (5.1 vs 10.3 per 1,000 views).

One caveat. Text-only videos are also much shorter (13 seconds for the middle one), and a short video has less time to rack up views. On-screen text still helps: TikTok's ad guide says 40% of the ads with the best watch-through use text overlays. So say the line and put a short version on screen, as 72% of the transcribed viral videos do. My breakdown of the 7 hook types has the details.

How to adapt a template without copying the video

Keep the sentence structure and change everything else: topic, example, visual. "If your salmon looks like this, you're doing it wrong" becomes "If your résumé looks like this, you're doing it wrong" for a career coach. Same promise: a visible mistake and its fix.

Try this tomorrow. Take your next video and write three hooks from three groups: an "if" scene, a question, a contrarian line. Check that each one says "you." Say the best one to camera in the first second and put a 6-word version on screen. TikTok tells advertisers to put the hook in the first 6 seconds and the core message in the first 3 (TikTok Ads help center, June 2025). My guide to how the TikTok algorithm works covers what gets measured next.

After 48 hours, compare shares and comments against your older videos with the engagement rate calculator. If views go up, the TikTok money calculator shows what they would pay. My take

Copy the mechanic, never the line. And if you change one word, change "I" to "you." In my data that single swap goes with 1.7 times the shares, and an "if" scene doubles them. They are the cheapest fixes there are: no gear, no editing. The limit is real. I only looked at videos that worked, and the "if" group is 16 hooks. I can't see the hundreds of "if" hooks that got 300 views because the video didn't deliver. A hook gets people in the door. The structure behind it keeps them, which is what my guide on how to go viral on TikTok is about.

Common questions

Do Instagram hooks work on TikTok and YouTube Shorts?

Yes. The videos I analyzed are mostly Instagram Reels, and every platform first checks whether people stay. Instagram lists the chance you watch a reel all the way through among its top predictions (May 2023). The same opening line works on Shorts.

How long should a hook be?

The spoken hook of the middle viral video is 10 words, and on-screen text is 6. In this list, the shortest template is one word ("Remember," 23.1 million views) and the longest is about 30. The promise matters more than the length.

Can I reuse another creator's hook?

A sentence pattern ("If your [X] looks like this…") moves freely between creators. Copying the whole video, its text and its edit word for word is another story, and your audience will notice. Keep the shape, bring your own topic, example and voice.
