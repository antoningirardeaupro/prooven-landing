Does Instagram pay you for views? What Reels really earn in 2026

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published October 26, 2026 · 8 min read

No. In October 2026, Instagram does not pay a set rate for Reels views, in the US or anywhere else. The only direct payouts are bonuses that Instagram's official bonus page calls "limited time and invite-only," and US creators need 5 million views a month for three months in a row to be considered. A typical viral Reel with 1.4 million views earns $0 from Instagram.

In short
  • Instagram publishes no per-view rate. Bonuses are invite-only, and the US threshold is 5 million monthly views held for three months.
  • Across nearly 500 viral Reels broken down one by one, the typical one got 1.4 million views. At TikTok's US rates it would earn about $280 to $565.
  • Only 21% of viral English-language Reels pass 5 million views on their own. Hitting that every month is rarer still.
  • 72% of these Reels got more views than the account has followers. That reach is what a brand pays for.

Does Instagram pay you for views in 2026?

Instagram doesn't pay for views unless it invites you. Its official bonus page says bonuses reward "the performance of your reels and photos" and are "limited time and invite-only." It lists no rate and no countries.

The Reels Play bonus that paid out big in 2021 stopped new and renewed deals on March 9, 2023. Since then, the Bonuses tab shows up in your professional dashboard only if Instagram picks you. On Reddit, on September 18, 2026, a creator with 19.9 million views on a single Reel put it bluntly: Instagram won't pay you.

A viral Reel with 1.4 million views earns $0 on Instagram. The same views would be worth roughly $350 to $425 on YouTube Shorts with a US audience.

Bonus thresholds by country

To get an invite, you have to hold a monthly view count for three straight months, and the US bar is the highest on the list. The table comes from Instagram's bonus rules page, as reproduced by Napoleoncat in September 2026.

The same rules name only Japan, South Korea and the US as places where you must live and pay tax, so the two lists don't fully match.

Even with an invite, a lot doesn't count. Branded, boosted and collab posts earn nothing. Each post earns for 28 days at most, and Instagram never states a rate. Creators in the photo bonus test, quoted by Miraflow in September 2026, report $0.14 to $0.16 per 1,000 views, a figure Meta hasn't confirmed.

What a viral Reel would earn if it were paid

A typical viral Reel would earn $0 on Instagram, $282 to $565 at TikTok's US rates, and $353 to $424 at YouTube Shorts rates. I took the views of the middle Reel among nearly 500 viral Reels broken down one by one (posted 2023 to July 2026, almost all since 2025). It has 1.41 million views.

The rates come from dated reports by creators with US audiences. TikTok pays $0.50 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views, and about 40% of views qualify. You also need videos over one minute and 10,000 followers (TikTok Creator Academy, September 11, 2026). YouTube Shorts pays about $0.25 to $0.30 per 1,000 views in the US, and AIR Media-Tech measured $0.33 in January 2026.

English-language Reels sit close to the overall number: 1.30 million views for the middle one, across about 340 Reels. Run your own numbers with the TikTok money calculator.

How many viral Reels clear the US bar

About one viral English-language Reel in five passes 5 million views by itself. That is the monthly US threshold, and you'd need to hit it three months running.

The UK bar of 500,000 is easier: 72% of viral Reels clear it alone. But these are hits, not normal months. An account with one viral Reel a quarter meets none of these thresholds. To see where your own numbers sit, check how many views counts as viral.

Instagram pays you in reach

A viral Reel mostly reaches people who don't follow you. For nearly 390 viral Reels, I know the account's follower count. 72% got more views than the account has followers, and 32% got more than 10 times as many.

Tenzin Zineon, a self-improvement creator, had 3,227 followers when I logged a 140-second Reel from the account, posted November 3, 2025. It got 2.25 million views. It opens with a question, not a claim:

"What if laziness is a habit of thinking about the cost or the effort instead of thinking about the payoff?"

Instagram paid nothing for those views. A 3,000-follower account that pulls 2 million views still has something to sell to a coaching or wellness brand.

What a brand deal is worth at the same views

One brand deal can out-earn a viral Reel's worth of per-view pay. Influencer Marketing Hub puts a micro-influencer's Instagram post at $150 to $500 in 2026. At YouTube Shorts US rates, $500 takes 1.7 to 2 million views, more than the typical viral Reel gets.

Agencies price by expected views. On a June 2026 Reddit thread, a US agency said it pays $0.02 to $0.10 per expected view, so $20 to $100 per 1,000. Shorts pays $0.25 to $0.30 per 1,000. That gap is 65 to 400 times.

Brands also check engagement. Viral English-language Reels average 6.5% engagement and 14 shares per 1,000 views, and self-improvement Reels reach 7.4%. Check yours with the engagement rate calculator, then read how much influencers make to price your first deal.

Other ways Meta pays creators

Outside bonuses, Instagram pays when your viewers pay. With Gifts, viewers send Stars on your Reels and you get $0.01 per Star, so 100 Stars is $1. You need 500 followers and to be 18 or older (Napoleoncat, May 2024). Subscriptions exist too, with monthly fans paying for exclusive content.

US creators have one more door. Meta's Breakthrough Bonus offers up to $5,000 extra during your first 90 days if you move over from another app, and it requires living in the US. Creators with 20,000 followers elsewhere get reviewed first. You can also repost Reels to Facebook, where monetization runs by invite.

If you want to be paid per view, TikTok and YouTube do it and Instagram doesn't. The guide on how to make money on TikTok walks through the conditions.

My take

Stop waiting for Instagram to send you a check. It pays in reach: 72% of the viral Reels I analyzed topped the account's follower count, and accounts with 10,000 to 50,000 followers reached 36 times their audience. That reach sells to brands per post, at rates per-view programs never come close to. If you're in the US and new to Instagram, the Breakthrough Bonus is the one direct payout worth applying for. My limit: I measure viral Reels, not your everyday views, and brand rates vary a lot. Take your last 10 Reels, sort them, keep the views of the 5th one, and lead with that number in your first pitch.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Instagram pay per 1,000 views?

Nothing, by default. Instagram publishes no rate. Invited creators in the photo bonus test report $0.14 to $0.16 per 1,000 views in 2026, unconfirmed by Meta.

How much does 1 million views on Instagram pay?

$0 directly. The same million views would earn about $200 to $400 on TikTok with a US audience (videos over one minute) and $250 to $300 on YouTube Shorts.

How many followers do you need to get paid on Instagram?

Bonuses look at monthly views, not followers. Gifts need 500 followers. Brands care about followers, but they care more about the views your normal Reels get.
