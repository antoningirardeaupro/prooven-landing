How to get unshadowbanned on TikTok, step by step (and why 36% of drops are normal)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published October 19, 2026 · 10 min read

To get unshadowbanned on TikTok, first confirm the restriction exists. Under the Community Guidelines in effect since September 24, 2026, TikTok shows any video removed from the For You feed in your analytics, and you can appeal it. Strikes drop off your record after 90 days. If you see no notice, your drop is probably normal: across 24 accounts that each had a viral video, 36% of videos got less than half the views of the one before.

In short
  • TikTok restrictions are visible: a note in a video's analytics, a notification when your whole account is affected, and Account Check, which audits your last 30 posts.
  • You have two official ways out: fix or delete the flagged video, or appeal. You track appeals in the in-app Safety Center.
  • TikTok publishes no "two-week" shadowban and no advice to stop posting. The only duration it gives is for strikes, which expire after 90 days.
  • A video under 10% of its account's usual level is rare: 3% of the 116 videos I tracked. Several in a row is the pattern worth acting on.

Step 1: look for the official notice

TikTok tells you itself when a video or your account is pulled from the For You feed. Its Community Guidelines, in effect since September 24, 2026, say that if your content is made ineligible for the feed or otherwise restricted, the information appears in TikTok's analytics tool. The same page adds that a video with few views has not necessarily broken a rule.

For your whole account, TikTok sends a notification. Since April 2024 it also has Account Check, which audits your account and your last 30 posts. It tells you whether you're in good standing and whether anything was removed. Do three things, in this order: open the analytics of your last 4 videos, read your notifications, then run Account Check from your settings. Menu names shift between app versions, so search the settings if you don't see it.

If all three come back clean, TikTok has no restriction on record. For the definition and the full check, read how to tell if you're really shadowbanned.

One line in the 2026 rules matters more than it looks. An account that breaks no rule can still become ineligible if it posts a lot of content that is. One flagged video rarely sinks your account. A string of them can.

How to appeal, and what to fix first

Appealing is the official route when you think TikTok got it wrong. The September 2026 rules cover every case: a banned account, a removed video, or a video made ineligible for For You or otherwise restricted. You appeal from the notice itself and then follow the status in the Safety Center inside the app.

Read the rule TikTok cites before you appeal. Two For You standards trip up creators who never meant to break anything. The first is "unoriginal or reused material without anything new," such as a clip from another account reposted as is. The second is content that tries to get people to send gifts or inflate likes or follows. If your video fits either one, edit or delete it. An appeal won't change the outcome.

If you post from the EU, the Digital Services Act gives you at least six months to file a free complaint against any decision that restricts your content's visibility (Article 20).

One video tanking proves nothing

Views swing wildly from one video to the next on the same account, with no restriction involved. I took the 24 accounts in our data with at least three dated videos each, 116 videos in total. Every one of them had at least one viral hit. Across 92 back-to-back pairs, the second video got less than half the views of the first 33 times. That's 36%, more than one in three.

The gap between an account's best and worst video tells the same story. It tops 2x for all 24 accounts and 10x for 15 of them.

For half of these accounts, the best video got more than 25 times the views of the worst. These are accounts that work.

A caveat: these are mostly Instagram Reels, because TikTok doesn't show other accounts' view history. Treat the numbers as a sense of how much normal reach swings, not as a TikTok-only measurement.

The post-viral trap

The drop that convinces most creators they're shadowbanned comes right after a viral video. In our data, the video posted after an account's best one gets 11% of its views on average. Of 21 accounts that posted again after their record, 18 never got back to half of it in the videos I have.

John runs a tech account with 29,906 followers. The account's Reel from December 18, 2025, hit 2.6 million views. The next five, from February 22 to March 19, 2026, landed between 8,357 and 28,754.

Next to the record, those videos are around 1% of the views. That looks like a blocked account. Next to the follower count, they reach 28% to 96% of the audience, which is an ordinary month for an account that size. Measure yourself against your usual level, never your record. The view thresholds by account size are in how many views is viral.

The drop that should worry you

One video under 10% of your usual level is rare, and several in a row are rarer still. Here's how to get your usual level: take your last 10 videos, sort them from fewest to most views, and keep the one in the middle.

About one video in five (18%) falls below half of its account's usual level, and 14 of the 24 accounts have at least one. That's a normal creator's life. Under 10%, only 3 videos out of 116 remain. Two come from the same account: Mr. High Level, a health page with 2.5 million followers. After three Reels between 17 and 56 million views in 2025, it got 467,388 and then 392,472 views on April 1 and 9, 2026. My data can't say whether the account was restricted. It does say that this pattern, several videos in a row far below normal, is the one that earns a second look.

Add a second check. In each video's analytics, look at the share of views from the For You feed. If it sits near zero on every video for two weeks, run Step 1 again, then appeal. If everything is clean, the problem is in the videos. The engagement rate calculator shows you which ones hold people the least.

Taking a break, mass deleting, starting over: what the rules say

Popular advice on this rests on no official page. Neal Schaffer's guide, revised August 11, 2026, says shadowbans "typically last around two weeks," then elsewhere "24 hours to a few days," and tells you to stop posting until you know the cause. None of it cites TikTok. The only number TikTok publishes is the 90-day strike expiry, from its February 2, 2023 announcement.

Deleting videos in bulk doesn't help either. Account Check tells you which post is the problem. Remove that one and keep the rest, which may still be picking up views. A new account sends you back to zero followers to escape a restriction that may not exist. Before that, see what videos that pass the first test round do in how to go viral on TikTok. If your latest video shows exactly 0 views, that's a different problem, covered in why your TikTok has 0 views.

Your plan for this week

The plan follows the evidence, strongest first. Budget about an hour.

  1. Monday: run Step 1. Analytics on your last 4 videos, notifications, Account Check.
  2. Found a notice? Read the rule it cites. Fix or delete the video if it fits, otherwise appeal and follow it in the Safety Center.
  3. No notice? Work out your usual level from your last 10 videos, record included.
  4. Count how many recent videos fall under 10% of that level. One means it's the video. Three in a row with For You traffic near zero means you run Step 1 again and appeal.
  5. Post your next video with a different hook and a different length. That video tells you whether your reach is back.

My take

In 2026, getting unshadowbanned on TikTok mostly means proving you were shadowbanned in the first place. TikTok's own rules require it to show restrictions in your analytics and let you appeal. The real trap is somewhere else. In my data, the video after a viral hit averages 11% of its views, and 18 of 21 accounts never got back to half their record. Any creator who measures against their best video will feel blocked almost every time. My position: without an official notice, don't take a break and don't start a new account. Change your next video. The limit: these numbers come mostly from Instagram Reels, and I don't have watch time or For You traffic for the accounts named here.

FAQ

How long does a TikTok shadowban last?

TikTok publishes no duration for a video or account that's ineligible for the For You feed. It only calls account-level restrictions "temporary." Strikes, on the other hand, expire from your record after 90 days.

Should I delete and repost the video?

Reposting the same video unchanged doesn't fix the cause. The For You standards also exclude reused material with nothing new added. Edit the part TikTok flagged, or appeal.

Can buying views restart my account?

No. TikTok's rules target fake engagement, and content that tries to inflate likes or follows isn't eligible for the For You feed. You'd be adding a real reason for a restriction to a problem that may not exist.
