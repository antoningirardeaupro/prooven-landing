TikTok FYP: how videos get on the For You Page (70% of viral videos use no hashtag)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 12, 2026 · 9 min read

A video gets on the TikTok For You Page (FYP) when TikTok predicts that strangers will watch it, finish it, like it or share it. TikTok spells this out on its official recommendation system page, checked in November 2026. The #fyp hashtag has nothing to do with it: of about 120 viral videos whose full caption I could read, 70% use no hashtag at all and only 3% carry #fyp or #foryou.

In short
  • The For You feed gives every video a score for every viewer, built from the interactions TikTok predicts. The highest scores get served.
  • Follower count and past hits are not "direct factors," according to TikTok since June 2020.
  • Some videos are barred from the FYP outright: reposts with nothing new added, posts that push for likes or gifts, and anything from accounts under 16. The current rules took effect on September 24, 2026.
  • In the viral videos I analyzed, length tracks views far more closely than hashtags. At 15 seconds or less, the typical video hit 2.6 million views, against 1.1 million past the one-minute mark.

What the For You Page actually is

The For You Page is the feed TikTok opens on. It mixes videos from accounts you follow with a lot of videos from accounts you have never seen. The other tab, Following, only shows people you follow.

For a creator, that split is the whole game. Your followers can find you in Following. Strangers only find you through For You. TikTok wrote on June 18, 2020 that neither follower count nor a history of high-performing videos is a direct factor in recommendations. So an account with 300 followers runs the same race as one with a million. If your videos are stuck near zero, the numbers on small accounts that broke through are in the guide on why your TikTok has 0 views.

How TikTok picks what goes on the FYP

TikTok scores every video for every person and serves the top scores. Its recommendation system page lays out the math in plain terms. The system predicts what you will do with a video: like it, share it, comment, or skip it. Those predictions roll up into one overall score, and the highest-scoring videos land in your feed.

To make those predictions, TikTok uses three groups of signals:

  • What you do: videos you watch, like, share, finish or skip, and the accounts you follow.
  • What the video contains: sound, length, hashtags, the creator's language, and when and where it was posted.
  • Your device: region, phone language, operating system.

The 2020 post adds a rule about weight. A strong signal counts more than a weak one, and TikTok's own example is watching a longer video all the way through. There is one last filter. If two very similar videos would play back to back, say with the same sound, TikTok swaps one out. That is why the hundredth copy of a trend loses to something different. The full breakdown of each signal is in how the TikTok algorithm works.

Videos TikTok keeps off the For You Page

A video can stay up on your profile and never reach the FYP. TikTok calls this being ineligible. The current rules were published on August 25, 2026 and took effect on September 24, 2026.

The first rule catches the most creators. Reposting someone else's clip as is keeps it off the FYP, even if it pulled millions of views on another account. TikTok says your video analytics will show you if one of your posts was made ineligible.

Does the #fyp hashtag do anything?

Almost no viral video uses it. I kept only the viral videos whose caption I could read in full, with nothing cut off, which left about 120. Seven in ten had no hashtag at all. Just 4 carried #fyp, #foryou or #foryoupage. That is 3%.

My favorite example is not even on TikTok. Emmet O'Brien posted an Instagram Reel on April 4, 2026 with a caption made of five tags: "#Fyp #viral #viral #foryoupage #fyp". It is a 9-second clip of two kids wiping out on a scooter and a mini dirt bike. It got 1.7 million views on an app that has no TikTok For You Page. The wipeouts did the work.

Compare Savannah Demers. Her video from November 20, 2025 has one line of caption, "never running out of dad jokes," and zero hashtags. It runs 11 seconds and has 25.4 million views. TikTok lists hashtags as video information, next to sound and length. They help it figure out what your video is about. No official TikTok page says a hashtag gets you onto the FYP.

Hashtags vs. no hashtags: views on viral videos

Viral videos without hashtags actually pulled slightly more views. In the same group of about 120, the typical no-hashtag video reached 1.3 million views, against 1.0 million for videos with at least one tag.

Format explains the gaps better than tags do. Videos with generic tags run 23 seconds: street interviews, pranks, rants to camera. Nearly half are entertainment. Videos with niche tags only run 61 seconds and are mostly tutorials, a third of them about business and money. People share them a lot, but to a smaller crowd.

In this group, no hashtag strategy did clearly better than using none at all. If #fyp opened the door to the For You Page, the 70% of viral videos that skip it would never have taken off.

English-language creators tag less than French ones: 19 of 67 English videos in this group have at least one hashtag, against 11 of 21 French videos. The French sample is too small to compare views. It mostly shows a habit.

The real ticket in: a video people finish

Length tracks views far more closely than hashtags. Across nearly 500 viral videos, half of those 15 seconds or shorter topped 2.6 million views. Past 60 seconds, the typical video sat at 1.1 million.

A short video is easier to finish. So it sends TikTok the strong signal it names itself more often. There is a flip side. Videos over 60 seconds have the best engagement rate, 6.8% for the typical one against 4.1% under 15 seconds. Long videos work too, as long as people stay to the end.

Your own engagement rate tells you which videos hold your audience. The engagement rate calculator works it out in a few seconds.

How to tell if your video reached the FYP

Open the video's analytics in TikTok Studio and look at where the views came from. The traffic sources split out For You, your profile, search and Following. If nearly everything came from people who already follow you, the video was never pushed to strangers.

That leaves two cases. Either the video was made ineligible, and TikTok flags it in your analytics. Or it was tested and did not hold attention, in which case nothing shows up and your next post starts fresh. The second case is far more common, and it feels like a shadowban without being one. How to tell them apart is covered in what a shadowban really is.

What changed in 2025 and 2026

Viewers now steer their own feeds, which shrinks your reach on some topics. Since June 6, 2025, the Manage topics tool lets anyone turn up or down more than 10 topics in their For You feed. TikTok says the setting never removes a topic completely.

In the US, the bigger change is ownership. TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC was announced on January 23, 2026 and is set to retrain the recommendation system on US user data, stored in Oracle's US cloud. No date has been given for when American feeds will run on the retrained version. The eligibility rules in the table above were rewritten on August 25, 2026 and apply to US creators too.

My take

Delete #fyp from your captions and spend those ten seconds on the first three seconds of your video. The data is clear: 70% of viral videos with a full caption use no hashtags at all, and the ones that do get no extra views. Length does track views: half of viral videos at 15 seconds or less topped 2.6 million. If you do add hashtags, pick two or three words your audience actually types into search, so TikTok can file your video correctly. The limit: my data comes mostly from Instagram Reels, and the full-caption sample is about 120 videos. I can't see watch time, and I don't see the videos that never took off. Frequently asked questions

What does FYP mean on TikTok?

FYP stands for For You Page, TikTok's main recommendation feed. When someone comments "fyp" under a video, they usually mean they found it there.

How long does it take to get on the For You Page?

TikTok publishes no timeline. The "first 24 hours" and "first 200 viewers" rules you see online cite no official source.

Do you need followers to get on the FYP?

No. TikTok has said since 2020 that follower count is not a direct factor. Accounts under 16, on the other hand, are never recommended.
