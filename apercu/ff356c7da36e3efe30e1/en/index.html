YouTube Shorts monetization: 10 million views, then about $0.33 per 1,000 in the US

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 14, 2026 · 8 min read

YouTube Shorts monetization requires 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, according to YouTube Help as read on November 4, 2026. A US audience then pays about $0.33 per 1,000 views (AIR Media-Tech, 2025 data). Starting February 1, 2027, dropping below 10 million views in 90 days cuts off your Shorts revenue, even if you're already a partner.

In short
  • At the 10 million threshold, a US audience earns you roughly $1,090 a month.
  • New applicants will need 20 million Shorts views in 90 days from February 1, 2027.
  • Only engaged views qualify, and YouTube excludes loops from them.
  • One English-language viral video in ten clears 10 million views alone. The typical one gets 1.33 million.
  • In creativity and content, you'd need 22 typical viral videos in 90 days to qualify.

YouTube Shorts monetization requirements right now

You need 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. The other route is 4,000 long-form watch hours in 12 months, and watch time from the Shorts feed doesn't count toward it.

A qualified view comes from a public Short and has to be engaged. YouTube's August 12, 2026 post spells it out: the viewer stayed past the first seconds, and loops are out. Views on private or deleted Shorts and on ads don't count either.

You also need a linked AdSense account, 2-Step Verification, advanced features, no active Community Guidelines strikes, and a home in an eligible country. Then there's a step people miss. You have to accept the Shorts Monetization Module, and views from before that date never pay.

A lower tier asks for 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and 3 million Shorts views. It opens memberships, Super Thanks and Shopping, with no ad revenue listed on the official page.

YouTube is splitting two questions: can you join, and can you get paid for Shorts. Google announced it on August 11, 2026.

The calendar is the catch. YouTube counts the 90 days ending on the 15th of the prior month. Your February 2027 earnings depend on the window that closes January 15, which opened around October 18, 2026. That clock is already running.

Fall short and you stay in the program, and long videos keep paying. Shorts revenue comes back once you clear the bar again. YouTube VP Amjad Hanif told Tubefilter the goal is keeping this money "meaningful" for creators. YouTube has also promised Shopping and brand-deal bonuses for channels below the line, without details so far.

Those are averages, and they hide a wide spread. AIR's own study puts most niches between $0.07 and $0.20, and a US-heavy channel near $300 per million views.

The bigger story is long-form. Across 274 channels, AIR found Shorts RPM runs 3 to 14% of long-form RPM in almost every niche. One million long-form views pay about $2,300. You need 11,000 to 34,000 Shorts views to match 1,000 long-form ones, and Shorts make up under 2% of revenue for most channels. Music and large kids' channels are the exceptions.

How the Creator Pool splits the money

Ads between Shorts go into a monthly pool, country by country. Part of it pays for music licensing. The rest is divided by your share of engaged views, and you keep 45% of what you're allocated.

Music won't lower your rate. A Short with one track sends half its revenue to the pool, and one with no music sends all of it. Your allocation still uses 100% of your engaged views either way, so stripping the sound doesn't raise your own payout.

Content ID is the rule to watch. A Short over one minute with an active claim gets blocked, and a blocked Short earns nothing. My breakdown of the Shorts algorithm covers how that plays out. Reuploads, compilations with nothing original added, and bought views are excluded too.

How many viral videos 10 million views takes

Seven or eight in 90 days, or one about every 12 days. That comes from nearly 500 viral vertical videos I analyzed, where the middle one has 1.41 million views. And these are videos that already worked.

Your niche decides a lot here. Lifestyle and entertainment needs 3 videos. Creativity and content needs 22, about one every 4 days.

Of 337 viral videos in English, one in ten clears 10 million views alone. Davide De Pierro's 66-second monologue from his car hit 25.3 million. At $0.328 that's about $8,300, if every view were engaged and American.

On paper, 4 fifteen-second videos do the work of 10 long ones. But a very short video lives on replays, and YouTube strips loops out of qualified views. The big public number on a 10-second Short tells you little about your threshold. YouTube doesn't publish a ratio between displayed and engaged views, and I don't have one.

Below the threshold, the money comes from elsewhere

Instagram has no open pay-per-view program for most creators, as I cover in whether Instagram pays for views. So on short video, brands do most of the paying. Numbers by account size are in how much influencers make.

That makes Shorts a discovery channel for most people, feeding something that pays better: a long video, a product, a sponsor.

My take

Treat Shorts ad revenue as a bonus. Even at $0.33, the threshold pays about $1,090 a month, and it takes 7 or 8 viral videos every 90 days to hold it. Long-form pays more than seven times as much per million views, on AIR's numbers. If your Shorts already clear 3.3 million engaged views a month, accept the terms and collect. If not, use Shorts to test formats and win sponsors, and put your ad revenue hopes on long videos. My limit: I have no Shorts in my data, and the RPM figures come from AIR Media-Tech's partner channels. Tonight, open YouTube Analytics, add up your Shorts' engaged views since October 18, and see where you stand.

Common questions

Do I have to reapply in 2027 if I'm already a partner?

No. You just have to accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027. YouTube says that's what lets you keep fully monetizing.

When does YouTube pay Shorts revenue?

Through AdSense. The pool is calculated monthly, and you'll see a daily estimate in YouTube Analytics.

Why is my RPM so low right after I get monetized?

Revenue sharing starts the day you accept the module, and earlier views don't pay. Give it a few weeks before you judge the number.
