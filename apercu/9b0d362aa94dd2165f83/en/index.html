How to turn on profile views on TikTok (and what a spike tells you)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 9, 2026 · 8 min read

To turn on profile views on TikTok, go to Settings and privacy, then Privacy, then Profile views, and switch on "Profile view history." TikTok then lists the accounts that visited your profile in the past 30 days. It only works if you are 16 or older with fewer than 5,000 followers, and only for visitors who turned the setting on too (TikTok Help Center, checked November 9, 2026).

In short
  • The setting is off by default and works both ways: once it's on, other people can see when you view their profile.
  • Once you hit 5,000 followers, the visitor list goes away. You keep a profile views count in TikTok Studio analytics.
  • A spike in profile views means a video reached people who don't follow you. Of the viral videos I analyzed from accounts under 100K followers, 76% were seen by more than 10 times the account's follower count.
  • Videos carried by a format travel furthest (7.1 views per follower), but videos carried by the person get twice the engagement (7.4% vs 3.6%).

How to turn on profile view history

It takes four taps in an up-to-date TikTok app, on iPhone or Android.

  1. Go to your profile and tap the three lines in the top right.
  2. Tap "Settings and privacy," then "Privacy."
  3. Tap "Profile views."
  4. Switch on "Profile view history."

TikTok lists two shortcuts. From your profile, tap the "Profile views" button at the top, then the settings icon. From your Inbox, tap Activities, then "Profile views," then the settings icon. All three routes land on the same switch, and you turn it off the same way. No "Profile views" menu anywhere? Your account probably misses one of the conditions below.

Who can see profile views: the age and follower rules

TikTok keeps the visitor list for small accounts. Its Help Center puts it in one line: "You must be 16 years or older and have fewer than 5,000 followers to have access to this feature." The list only goes back 30 days. TikTok told TechCrunch in June 2023 that the feature was available globally.

TikTok doesn't explain the 5,000 cap. My guess: picture a 50K account. It gets thousands of visits a day, and a list of usernames would tell it nothing. TikTok hands it a number instead.

Why your visitor list looks empty

You only see visitors who switched the setting on themselves. Everyone else passes through without a trace, and since the setting starts off, most people never show up.

The rule is mutual. Turning it on means your own visits show up for anyone who has it on. Check out an ex's profile or a rival creator's, and they may see your name. TikTok's Help Center describes no other way to see your visitors. Third-party apps that promise "see who viewed your TikTok" mostly want your login. Don't give it to them.

So don't treat that list as audience data. It's a self-selected sample. The real number lives in your analytics.

The profile views count everyone gets

Your profile views count lives in analytics and has nothing to do with the history setting. Open TikTok Studio from your profile, then Analytics. The Overview tab shows profile views next to post views, likes, comments and shares. You pick the range: the last 7, 28, 60 or 365 days, or a custom report (TikTok Creator Academy).

That number is worth more than the list of names. It tells you how many people wanted to know more about you after watching something. Put it next to your new followers for the same period. If 2,000 people viewed your profile and 40 followed, that's 2 in 100. This ratio grades your profile (photo, bio, pinned videos). Views grade your videos.

TikTok publishes no average for how many profile visitors become followers. Track your own number week over week.

A spike in profile views means strangers found you

When profile views jump overnight, one of your videos left your follower bubble. Your followers already know your profile. Strangers from the For You feed are the ones tapping your name.

My data shows how much virality runs on strangers. Out of nearly 390 viral videos where I know the account's follower count, 72% got more views than the account had followers. A third (33%) got more than 10 times that. The middle video sits at 4.2 views per follower.

The gap widens with success. Videos I tagged "mega-viral" sit at 33 views per follower for the middle video. Videos that simply did well sit at 1.5. The ones that stayed average sit at 0.3, which means they barely left the follower base. For the view counts that actually count as viral, see how many views is viral.

A simple benchmark: views divided by followers

Divide a video's views by your follower count. Above 10, mostly strangers watched it, and that's when profile views should spike. Below 1, it stayed with your followers.

For a small account, going viral almost always looks like this. Three in four viral videos from accounts under 100K followers passed 10 times their follower count. For accounts over 1M, it's 6%. A big account can go viral without leaving home. You can't. Every video that works sends strangers to your profile, and that's your one shot at keeping them.

A real case: Zoe Emma Hill had about 30,000 followers when I analyzed her December 2025 Reel. The on-screen hook read "In Japan, Laziness is considered a Disease and people treat it with these 7 methods." It pulled 28 million views, more than 900 per follower.

Formats bring visitors, people keep them

Not every profile visit is worth the same. Videos carried by a format travel furthest, while videos carried by the person get the strongest reaction. For each video, I logged what made it work: the format (an animated infographic, a ranking), the script (the hook, the idea) or the person on camera.

Format-driven videos hit 7.1 views per follower, more than double the person-driven ones (3.1). Their engagement rate, though, drops to 3.6%, against 7.4% when the person carries the video.

Zoe's Reel is a format video: a bold text hook, with the seven methods left in the caption. That kind of post pulls a crowd to your profile who liked the post, not necessarily you. I don't track followers gained per video, so treat this as a hypothesis. It matches what I found on how to get more followers on TikTok: the bigger the account, the more its viral videos rely on the person.

What to do when profile views spike

A visitor makes up their mind in seconds, from the top of your profile. Get it ready before the spike.

  1. Rewrite your bio as one line on what people get by following: "one real estate tip a day" says more than "real estate enthusiast."
  2. Pin three videos: your best performer, one where people see your face, and part 1 of a series.
  3. Every Sunday, log profile views and new followers for the week from TikTok Studio, and work out the ratio.
  4. After a video that tops 10 times your follower count, post a follow-up within 48 hours with the same hook. Yesterday's visitors will recognize it.

If profile views climb while that ratio drops, fix your bio and pinned videos before you touch your content. To check whether your current followers still react, run your numbers through the engagement rate calculator.

My take

Turn on profile view history if you're under 5,000 followers and curious, but don't make decisions with it. The list only shows visitors who made the same choice you did, and it exposes your own visits in return. The number that matters is the profile views count, set against new followers. My data explains why: for accounts under 100K, three in four viral videos mostly reach strangers. Each spike is a line of strangers at your door, and your profile decides whether they come in. The limit: I only analyzed videos that worked, mostly on Instagram, with follower counts taken when I analyzed them. I can see how far a video traveled, not how many visitors it turned into followers.

Frequently asked questions

Can someone tell I looked at their TikTok profile?

Only if you both have profile view history on and they have fewer than 5,000 followers. Otherwise your visit stays hidden.

Does TikTok show who watched my videos?

No. You see a view count per video, never names. Profile view history only covers visits to your profile.

Why did the profile views option disappear?

Most often you reached 5,000 followers. Update the app too, since the menu moves between versions.

Do profile views help with the algorithm?

TikTok doesn't say. What it does say is that it recommends each video based on how viewers react to it. More in my guide to how the TikTok algorithm works.
