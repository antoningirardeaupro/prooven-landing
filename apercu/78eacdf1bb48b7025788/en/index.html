How long should a TikTok be? 19 to 60 seconds, depending on format

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published November 25, 2026 · 8 min read

A TikTok should be as long as its format needs. Across more than 1,500 viral videos analyzed by Prooven (snapshot of July 29, 2026), half run 37 seconds or less, but a viral self-improvement video runs 60 seconds at the midpoint, versus 31 for lifestyle. To earn from the Creator Rewards Program, a video must be over 1 minute (TikTok Creator Academy, September 2026). TikTok accepts uploads up to 60 minutes.

In short
  • One viral video in four lasts 14 seconds or less, and one in ten runs past 1:42. The longest one we found is 4:39.
  • Comedy is the shortest format: the middle viral comedy video runs 19 seconds. Educational videos sit around 60 seconds.
  • In English, viral videos of 30 seconds or less get the most views: 1.8 million for the middle video, versus 866,000 past 90 seconds.
  • Past 90 seconds, English-language viral videos get shared 2.2 times more per view than short ones.

TikTok video length limits in 2026

You can post anything from 1 second to 60 minutes. TikTok states the upper limit on its official Creator Rewards page: uploads "up to a whopping 60 minutes long" on TikTok.com. The in-app camera offers shorter presets, and the options can vary by account.

The number that matters most is 60 seconds. The Creator Rewards Program only pays for original videos "over 1 minute long." A video that stops at exactly 1:00 earns nothing. With a US audience, creators on r/TikTokMonetizing report roughly $0.50 to $1 per 1,000 qualified views in 2026, so those extra seconds can be worth real money. The full rules (1,000 For You views, no Duets, no Photo Mode) are in my guide to the TikTok Creator Rewards Program. The rest of this article is about views.

How long viral videos actually are

Half of the viral videos I've analyzed are 37 seconds or shorter. That figure covers the full database, more than 1,500 videos as of July 29, 2026. A quarter fit in 14 seconds. On the long end, a quarter go past 67 seconds, and one in ten past 1:42.

The subset I broke down in detail (about 500 videos, with hook, format and structure tagged) skews longer: 52 seconds at the midpoint, with 43% over a minute. Different videos, and only this subset has niche and format data, so every niche figure below comes from it.

Put together, the spread is huge. Viral videos range from a few seconds to more than 4 minutes. No length guarantees anything, but each niche has a comfort zone.

Niches that explain things (money, health, mindset) hover around a minute. Niches that show or entertain stay under 45 seconds. Lifestyle has the shortest videos and the biggest view counts, driven by skits that last a few seconds. If you run a finance account, copying a comedy creator's length won't help you.

Short for laughs, long for lessons

Format sets the length before niche does. Viral videos built on humor run 19 seconds at the midpoint, and half of them clear 4.1 million views. Educational videos run 60 seconds, and storytelling and testimonials run 61.

A joke lives on its punchline, and punchlines don't stretch. Gus Johnson's 11-second skit about an everyday situation, posted December 5, 2025, pulled 30.9 million views on Instagram. There's barely time to scroll away.

A lesson needs room to set up the problem, prove it and land the answer. Jeffrey Meltzer took 112 seconds for a numbered list:

"10 Habits That Worsen Your Mental Health"
Jeffrey Meltzer, 112-second video posted April 5, 2025, 4.7 million views on Instagram.

Demos land in between, at 38 seconds: you show, you don't narrate. Pick the format first and the length follows.

In English, the shortest videos get the most views

Among English-language viral videos, 30 seconds or less is the length that gets the most views. Past 90 seconds, the middle video gets half as many.

The middle of the chart is the trap. Between 31 and 60 seconds, one video in four stays under 226,000 views, the lowest floor of any group. Too long to loop, too short to teach anything worth sending. If your video lands at 45 seconds, either cut it to 25 or build it out past a minute.

Long videos still break out. The most viewed English-language video over 90 seconds in my set is a 3-minute escalating experiment by the creator Ross, posted December 24, 2025: 72.5 million views. It works because every step raises the stakes, so nobody leaves before the payoff.

French-speaking creators show the opposite pattern. In that smaller group (about 70 videos), viral videos over 90 seconds get 2.5 times the views of those under 30. The rule changes with the audience.

Longer TikToks get shared more

Length trades views for shares. Past 90 seconds, English-language viral videos get 17.5 shares per 1,000 views, versus 7.8 at 30 seconds or less.

In health and wellness, viral videos over 90 seconds get 22 shares per 1,000 views. Videos of 30 seconds or less get 6. That's 3.6 times more sharing for the long version.

People forward a sleep routine to their partner. They don't forward a 20-second tip they forgot by lunch. In my breakdown of how the TikTok algorithm works, shares were the only visible signal that raised the view floor. One exception: in business and finance, videos over 90 seconds stall at 384,000 views for the middle video. On money topics, a minute is enough.

The big TikTok studies say longer videos get more views, and for an average account they're right. They measure a different thing.

In Buffer's data, a 5 to 10 second TikTok reaches a median of 194 people. At that scale, a long video holds the few viewers it gets for longer and racks up more. My videos went viral: three in four cleared 450,000 views. At that level, what decides is whether people finish and send it. Both findings hold. They answer different questions.

How to find the right length for your account

Your ideal length is in your own analytics. Open a video's analytics: TikTok shows average watch time and the share of viewers who watched the full video. Divide average watch time by the video's length, and do it for your last 10 posts.

If people drop at the same moment every time, cut before it. If your long videos keep half their viewers past the midpoint, you can hold a minute. Then test one format at two lengths, say 25 and 70 seconds, over two weeks. Compare shares, not only views; the engagement rate calculator does the math. Post both at similar times (my guide to the best time to post on TikTok helps), or you'll end up testing the clock instead of the length.

A long video lives or dies in its first seconds. TikTok says watch time is "a key factor" for the Additional Reward and recommends a hook within the first 5 seconds. My guide to TikTok hooks breaks down the openers that hold.

My take

If you post in English, go short or go past the minute, and skip the 45-second middle. A skit or a single punchline belongs under 30 seconds, where English-language viral videos get the most views. A lesson about money, health or habits belongs at 61 to 90 seconds: you keep most of the reach, people share it more, and TikTok pays you for it. The limit: my database is mostly Instagram Reels, and I can't see watch time on other people's videos. I know which lengths went viral, not how long viewers stayed.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 60-second TikTok get paid?

No. TikTok's page says "over 1 minute." Aim for 65 seconds in the edit to stay safe.

Do 10-minute TikToks work?

My data can't tell you: the longest viral video in the database runs 4:39. Socialinsider sees brand views dip slightly past 3 minutes.

How much does a 1-minute TikTok make?

It depends on your qualified views and where your audience lives. The TikTok money calculator estimates it from your numbers.
