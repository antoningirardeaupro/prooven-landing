How much do influencers make in 2026? 67% earn under $10K a year (by follower count)

By Antonin Girardeau, founder of Prooven · Published October 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Most influencers make less than $10,000 a year: that is 67% of the 5,055 creators CreatorIQ surveyed in June 2026, mostly in the US, and only 4% clear $100,000. Per brand deal, the money scales with followers: from $150 to $800 a post for micro creators (Influencer Marketing Hub, July 2026) to about $14,600 for accounts over 1 million followers working with a European agency.

In short
  • CreatorIQ, 2026: 67% of creators earn under $10,000 a year from content, and 62% say it is not their main income.
  • In nearly 390 viral videos I broke down, accounts over 1 million followers have 80 times the followers of 10K to 50K accounts, but their typical viral video gets only 2 times the views.
  • So a brand pays about $3.65 per 1,000 viral views with a micro creator and about $8 with a mega creator.
  • For accounts where I have 3 or more videos, the typical video gets 17% of the views of the best one. Price yourself on the typical one.

What the average influencer actually earns

The typical influencer earns pocket money, not a salary. CreatorIQ and Influencers.club surveyed 5,055 creators from May 29 to June 29, 2026, with 63% in the US, 14% in the UK and 9% in Canada. Two-thirds made under $10,000 a year from content. Only 4% made more than $100,000.

The same survey has a quieter finding. For 62% of respondents, content is not their main source of income. So when you see a headline about a creator buying a house, you are looking at the top 4%.

France tells the same story with different numbers. The Reech study (reported November 16, 2025) found that 26% of French influencers earn nothing and 56% make under €1,000 a month.

One creator in four makes zero. Fewer than one in twenty clears $100,000 a year (Reech 2025, CreatorIQ 2026).

A sponsored post pays anywhere from $150 to about $14,600, and the published rate cards disagree. Here are the most recent ones with real numbers side by side.

The gap at the micro level is huge: $150 versus $3,400. Matriochka negotiates for big beauty brands like Guerlain and Eucerin. Influencer Marketing Hub describes the whole market, including the creator who emails a small brand on their own. If that is you, plan on the left column first.

Followers grow 80 times, views grow 2 times

A 2.4 million follower account does not get 80 times the views of a 30,000 follower one. For nearly 390 viral videos I broke down one by one, I know the account's follower count. Between 10K and 50K followers, the typical viral video gets 0.92 million views. Over 1 million followers, it gets 1.83 million.

Followers go up 82 times between those two tiers. Views go up 2 times. The Matriochka price goes up 4.3 times. Brands pay for more than reach, but much less than follower count.

Tiny accounts are the outliers. Under 10K followers, the typical viral video hit 1.9 million views, on just 14 videos. Amelie OOTDs had 4,439 followers when a 28-second fashion Reel reached 7.9 million views on November 3, 2025. If you want to see where your own numbers sit, I mapped the tiers in how many views counts as viral.

What a brand really pays per 1,000 views

A brand pays about $3.65 per 1,000 viral views with a micro creator, and about $8 with a mega creator. The math: the Matriochka median price, divided by the views of the typical viral video in each tier.

Treat these as a floor. A brand pays for the views it expects, and a sponsored post rarely does viral numbers. In a June 2026 Reddit thread, a US agency said it pays $0.02 to $0.10 per expected view. That is $20 to $100 per 1,000 views, because it counts normal views, not the record.

For a small creator, this is good news. View for view, you cost a brand half what a star costs. Put that in your first pitch email.

Your best video is not your rate

For a single creator, the typical video gets 17% of the views of their best one. I have 3 or more videos for 23 accounts, a little over 110 videos in total. For 70% of those accounts, the best video did more than 3 times their usual views.

Quote a rate based on your record and the brand will notice by the first post. Take your last 10 videos instead, sort them by views, and keep the 5th or 6th. That number is what a brand is buying.

Say your typical video gets 60,000 views. At the agency's $20 to $100 per 1,000, that is $1,200 to $6,000 per video. On a first deal you negotiate alone, start near the bottom and raise it after each campaign that delivers. Brands will also ask for your engagement, and the engagement rate calculator gives you that in ten seconds.

What the platforms pay on top

Platforms pay 9 to 18 times less than a brand for the same views. TikTok's Creator Rewards Program pays about $0.50 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views for a US audience in 2026, based on dated creator reports. Around 40% of views qualify, so that works out to $0.20 to $0.40 per 1,000 total views. A micro brand deal sits at $3.65.

And you have to qualify first: 10,000 followers, 100,000 views in the last 30 days, age 18, and only videos over 1 minute get paid (TikTok Creator Academy, updated September 11, 2026). The follower side is covered in how many followers you need to get paid on TikTok, the full playbook in how to make money on TikTok, and you can run your own numbers in the TikTok money calculator.

My take

Ask how much influencers make and the honest answer is: almost nothing, unless brands pay them. One million TikTok views earns a US creator $200 to $400 if the video runs past a minute. Sold to a brand, the same audience is worth thousands. And micro creators are the best deal a brand can get, at about $3.65 per 1,000 viral views. If you have 20,000 followers, stop waiting to "get bigger" and start pitching. The limit of my math: I measured viral videos, mostly on Instagram, not your everyday views. That is exactly why your rate should come from your typical video, never your best one.

Common questions

How much do influencers make with 10K followers?

At 10K followers, expect $150 to $500 per Instagram post and $200 to $800 per TikTok video, per Influencer Marketing Hub (July 2026). Two deals a month is a side income, not a job. How many deals you land matters as much as the rate.

How much do influencers make with 1 million followers?

Over 1 million followers, the Matriochka median was about $14,600 per deal in the first half of 2026. That price did not move from 2025, while micro creator prices tripled over the same year.

Do followers still matter?

Yes, more than brands admit. In the CreatorIQ survey, brands ranked follower count last of eight criteria. Yet on Instagram it was the single metric most linked to what creators actually earned.
